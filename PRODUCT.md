# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Small-business owners in the Ottawa–Toronto corridor who need a professional website but do not want to build or maintain it themselves. They arrive skeptical of agencies and template platforms; they need enough trust to send one short inquiry.

## Product Purpose

MiviaLab’s marketing site exists to earn trust and convert strangers into a single primary contact action (short project form; WeChat secondary). Success is qualified outreach — not vanity traffic. The site must prove craft (speed, security, custom code, full service) without inventing clients or testimonials.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, premium — a Canadian studio that feels considered rather than loud. Voice is plainspoken and buyer-outcome focused (“loads fast so you stop losing customers”), never niche-labeled or salesy. Emotional goal: quiet confidence that this partner will handle the site end to end.

## Anti-references

- “Chinese niche” or community-first public branding (community is GTM wedge, not identity)
- Template-platform aesthetics (Wix/Squarespace brochure look)
- Fake client logos, invented testimonials, or unlabeled concept work presented as live clients
- Equal-weight contact menus (six channels, no funnel)
- Generic AI-default marketing chrome: purple gradients, cream-paper cliché used as the whole identity, dashboard-like heroes

## Design Principles

1. **Trust before contact** — every section either proves capability or removes doubt; the page ends in one clear ask.
2. **Practice what you preach** — the site itself should feel fast, custom, and carefully maintained.
3. **Show real work honestly** — one real client (Meng Wei Yue Opera Studio); samples always labeled as concepts.
4. **Buyer outcomes over jargon** — lead with speed, security, custom build, and “you never touch the site.”
5. **Canadian studio, bilingual as capability** — English-first Ottawa positioning; EN / FR / 中文 as a premium feature, not the brand.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA on interactive and content surfaces. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for splash, parallax, and scroll reveals. Maintain readable contrast on both the dark cinematic hero and light editorial body. Form errors must be text-visible, not color-only.
